Three spawners, one gate, and a minute pass that also keeps house

Nothing resident supervises the fleet. Every worker respawns itself and revives its peers, WP-Cron catches a fleet with nothing left running, and all three cross the same throttled endpoint, so N spawners are as safe as one.

Tier 1 · every worker, itself

The finally in Worker_Base::execute()

release() first, then self_respawn(), so the successor can take the lock at once. Runs at the end of every ~595 s lifetime; the slot changes hands immediately instead of idling until a rescue.

Reversed, the successor's acquire hits the still-held lock and skips.

Tier 1 · every worker, its peers

_fleet (Fleet_Node), every 15 s

SCAN_INTERVAL_MS. Spawns any fleet worker whose lock dir is missing or whose heartbeat has aged past its stale_timeout, at most MAX_SPAWNS_PER_TICK (4) per pass: each POST is a blocking cURL inside a drain loop, so a cold fleet spreads its spawns over consecutive passes rather than stalling one.

Tier 2 · WP-Cron, once a minute

Bootstrap::reconcile_fleet()

Catches a fleet with nothing left running: with every worker dead there is nothing left to scan, so a total fleet death waits up to a cron minute. One pass that revives first and then keeps house, with no per-pass cap.

Which is why the cold-start pass has its own direct tests.

The one gate: is_recently_spawned, 15 s per slot

The spawn endpoint enforces and records the throttle, because it is the one gate every spawner crosses. An accepted spawn writes last_spawn:{type}|{partition} through Cache_Backend::shared_first(), falling back to a transient, at a TTL of twice MIN_SPAWN_INTERVAL_S so the record outlives the window it guards and expires on its own. A slot spawned under 15 s ago is refused, so respawn storms are bounded however many spawners there are.

The minute pass, Bootstrap::run_reconcile_steps(): seven steps in a fixed order, each alone behind its own try/catch

1before_reconcileA third-party hook, isolated because a throw from it fired bare would escape the callback and skip the spawn behind it.
2spawn_due_workers()Revival, ahead of every janitorial step.
3wake_readers_with_backlog()The only pass that notices an external producer's write.
4lock-dir reconcile
5retention sweepsLog_Cleaner's delete grace alone is an hour.
6orphan-IPC reaping
7newspack_nodes/periodicAlerts, the delayed-jobs sweep and every subscriber; not_before granularity is therefore a minute, and late is what it means.

No step can cost the others their window. Housekeeping depends on no live worker at all: retention and orphan reaping run while the fleet is down, which is when disk most needs reclaiming.

Rejected: a dedicated supervisor process as the middle tier

It is no supervisor in the OS sense: it can neither signal a worker, reap it, nor restart it in place. All it does is poll lock-dir mtimes and POST to an HTTP endpoint, and polling is work the pollees do for each other. Revival must not depend on a single process; peer scanning honors that more completely while giving back a permanently resident PHP-FPM child. Self-respawn alone was rejected too, since nothing catches a worker that dies before it can respawn, and an OS-level supervisor is unavailable on the platform.

What N independent spawners buy

Supervision survives the loss of any single process rather than dying with one. The throttle that makes three spawners safe makes N safe. Revisit only if an OS-level process supervisor becomes available, at which point the tiered self-revival collapses into it.
