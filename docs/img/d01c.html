Durable state is a log on disk, and a hub reads it from far away

One primitive carries the whole pipeline: an append-only partition of packed messages in numbered segment files. Locally the readers are nodes in the same process. Across sites the reader is a hub, pulling the same partition over SSE under a cursor it owns.

Reader

Consumer

Reads from its cursor, unpacks each record into a message, fills its sink, and advances.

Checkpoint: the position goes to offsets/…, a tiny second partition called the offsetlog. A recycled worker resumes from the newest entry.

Poison: a record that makes the reader throw goes to deadletter/… and the cursor moves past it. A job that throws is dead-lettered the same way.

The 4 KB rule is what removes the lock

A record stays under 4 KB, the size a POSIX append lands atomically, so many PHP requests write one firehose log at once and none of them coordinates. Every field a record carries competes for that budget.

Retention prunes oldest-first, by three rules

An age limit deletes segments older than a lifetime. A target segment count deletes down to that target, sparing any segment younger than a minimum lifetime. A hard cap, twice the target by default, deletes above it whatever the ages are.

Hub: one topology, one worker

One Remote_Source node per spoke. Each opens that spoke's SSE stream endpoint and pulls its firehose partition into a partition of its own on the hub's disk, one per spoke, where the hub's dashboards read it.

Each holds its own durable cursor, so a restart resumes where the hub stopped rather than where the spoke did.

Vault

The operator enters each spoke's URL and password once. The topology line carries the entry's id and nothing else, so the graph is safe to read and to check in.

Pulling is what makes flow control free

The hub asks for what it can take. When its buffer crosses 512 KB it stops reading the socket, the TCP window closes on the spoke, and nothing is dropped: the spoke keeps its log until the hub comes back for it.
