Where an ingested record lands, and what the destination keeps

Every record read from a dead-letter or partition segment goes back in through Topic_Node::fill(). The destination's shape is decided once, from the <topic> argument and three flags; each record's partition is decided per record, from its own envelope.

1. The destination is taken as written

A bare log namefirehose expands to <config:logs_dir>/firehose.p<partition>, with the configured num_partitions.
A dir-templateCarries a <partition> or {partition} token and is used verbatim once its <config:…> tokens resolve. It defaults to ONE partition, because the template names a layout already on disk.

Neither form is checked against the declared log set

wp nodes ingest firehoze … builds firehoze.p0/, replays into it and ends with Ingested N record(s). Nothing declares that dir, so the orphan sweep deletes it an hour after the last write, or wp nodes gc --force at once. The Destination: line the command prints first is the only confirmation the replay landed where you meant.

2. Each record picks its own partition

A pin past the destination's count — a p3 replayed into a two-partition Topic — is out of range and falls through to the KEY hash, or to round-robin when it carries no KEY, rather than failing, so a replay into a narrower Topic still lands. A line that will not unpack is counted and skipped, so a torn record at a segment's tail cannot abandon the replay. A filtered wp nodes reqgrep or a zcat pipes in on stdin when no file is named.

Overriding any of the three is what makes re-segmenting an existing log the same operation as a replay. The three geometry flags refuse zero, a sign, a leading zero and a suffix.

The window trap: a replay prunes its own head

Age retention is off on purpose — every re-ingested segment carries a fresh mtime — but min_lifetime at 0 also strips the count rule's age floor, so the destination prunes its oldest segment the moment it holds more than num_segments.

wp nodes ingest firehose old/*.log --segment_size=1048576 --num_segments=2
seg 0, pruned
seg 1, pruned
seg 2, pruned
seg 3, 1 MiB
seg 4, 1 MiB

The destination holds 2 MiB and nothing more: a replay longer than that deletes its earlier output as it proceeds and keeps only the tail. Restoring a whole log means sizing --num_segments and --segment_size to span the source, then judging the destination against the source rather than assuming it complete.

4. Records above the 4 KB PIPE_BUF cap

--allow_large_writes

A held per-partition lock, taken lazily when the first record routes to each partition. Against a partition a live writer holds it stalls 15 seconds, then aborts the replay with a fatal — after the records already routed have been accepted and flushed.

--void_warranty

No lock; the caller asserts single-writer. It cannot stall or abort this way, which is the trade the assertion buys. Either flag raises the cap to 32 MiB. Passing both is refused.

--dry-run

Writes nothing, reports the largest record it saw, and says which of the two flags you need. Without either flag an oversize record is skipped and counted, never written torn.
