A browser's command to a worker rides one POST, and its reply comes home through a pid-bound gate on the stream

Every tab attached to one worker consumes that worker's output Partition, so without a gate each would receive every other tab's replies. One SSE stream process serves one session, its pid names the session, and HTTP_Filter_Node is the _output boundary of that process. Watch FROM grow on the way out and TO shrink on the way back.

The console at cwd job-worker.p0: a RemoteIpcNode per worker, one open stream per session.

5The subscription Consumer replays the record into _default_route, whose _sse target stamps only an empty TO, and on through the interpreter into _router, which peels _output and fills HTTP_Filter_Node. The gate bumps its counter, splits TO at the first /, and compares the WHOLE head against its own _sse:<pid> — the colon keeps the pid inside one segment, where _sse/<pid>/… would leave a bare _sse head and drop every reply. A match strips the head and forwards; anything else is dropped silently, an unaddressed broadcast as much as another session's reply.TO _sse:4242/_output → _output
6The tab's own Router dispatches on _output and the transcript renders it. A polling node's reply lands the same way — _sse:4242/_metadata — which is why canonicalReverseCwd() rewrites that last segment to _output before the Inspector compares a _header.pwd against a shell connect_node.

The gate closes the READ direction alone

A process admits only replies bearing its own getmypid(), so no client can make its filter accept another session's traffic. But the head is client-composed — Command_Auth::canonical() signs semantics and never routing — so what stands between an ordinary user and another operator's console is the MANAGE default on connect_worker_input, not this filter. A climbing ls -c on _output in a stream process reports what the gate SAW, not what it passed.

Two corollaries of a whole-head compare

Core's default stderr handler picks its sink _repl ?? _sse ?? _output. Inside a stream process both _sse and _output are mounted, so an empty-TO stderr line handed to _output is swallowed here and reaches the browser only through error_log().

Only ONE stream is open per browser session: a send on a second worker's link closes whichever RemoteIpcNode holds the live one (RemoteIpcNode.active), because SSE slots are a finite host-wide pool. PHP has no Remote_IPC and steals nothing — each Remote_Source patrons its own HTTP_Out, so several links connect at once.
