What a slot can be, and who puts it back

Every row of wp nodes status is one slot in one of five states. Three spawners drive the arrows between them, and none of the three depends on any one process staying alive.

1
The routine recycle. The runtime expires, the worker releases its lock and POSTs its own successor.
2
A death before the finally. The lock directory outlives the process and its heartbeat ages past the topology's stale timeout.
3
Nothing holds the slot. A resident topology with no lock directory is due immediately.
4
Due for revival. A peer scan or the cron pass finds the slot and POSTs a spawn for it.
5
A new process takes the lock, rebuilds the graph, and resumes every consumer from its offsetlog.
6
Every consumer sat at the end of its log for the whole idle window, so the worker exits holding no lock and declines its own respawn.
7
A wake. Idle is not due for revival, so only a wake ends it. A stale lock still spawns, because a stale lock is a crash.
8
wp nodes stop writes the hold as an option, so a reinstall that wipes the base directory leaves it standing. The endpoint answers 409 while it stands.
9
wp nodes start clears the hold and spawns every due slot.
●
Every row carries a heartbeat age and an uptime. Uptime that resets every ten minutes is health rather than a crash loop, because it is arrow 1 doing its work.

Tier one: every worker watches its peers

The _fleet node inside each live worker scans every peer's lock directory every 15 s and POSTs a spawn for each slot it finds due.

A pass spawns at most four, because each POST blocks a drain loop that has messages waiting. A cold fleet therefore comes up over consecutive passes.

A scan that throws logs the error once and swallows it, so one bad topology provider cannot crash every worker in lockstep.

Tier two: newspack_nodes/reconcile on WP-Cron, every minute, running Bootstrap::reconcile_fleet()

The cold start. With every worker dead there is nothing left to scan, so a total fleet death waits up to a minute.

1
Spawn every due slot, under no cap.
2
Wake each on-demand reader holding a backlog.
3
Retire lock directories outside the active set.
4
Sweep orphan partitions.
5
Fire the periodic hook.

Each step runs behind its own catch, and none needs a live worker, so retention runs while the fleet is down.

The one row to read first

wp nodes doctor renders eight rows, and a critical row exits non-zero.

Its housekeeping row asks whether the reconcile event is scheduled. When it is not, retention, alerts and cold-start revival all stop while every other check stays green. The row names the recovery command.
