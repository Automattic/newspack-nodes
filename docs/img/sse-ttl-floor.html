The lease floor is three heartbeats, because a re-auth costs one of them

Only the client's own poke refreshes a lease; the server checks and extends nothing. A client that loses its session stops poking for the whole re-auth round trip, so a TTL sized for a lost poke alone fences a stream that is merely re-authenticating, and costs the reader its slot at the moment it is least able to reclaim one.

One machine pull's lease across a re-auth, on a 60-second TTL

Whose poke it is

The lease a client pokes arrives in the connected envelope, which carries SLOT and OWNER beside the stream's PID and cursors. A browser pokes workers heartbeat <slot> <owner> from its _heartbeat node every 15 s (POKE_INTERVAL_MS), one poke per live lease; a machine pull pokes it from Remote_Link_Node on the same cadence.

_heartbeat is one per page, not one per stream: each link registers its own lease under its own identity and drops it on close, so a page holding several streams spends several of its identity's slots and refreshes them all in one tick.

Only an owner-matched poke refreshes; check() never does. A stream lives exactly as long as its client keeps saying so, however long the connection stays open.

Why the ask is phased by name

The connect queue spreads first boot alone. A spoke restart or a key rotation drops every link's session at once, leaving every link past its retry gate and asking together. A phase on the absolute clock, from the link's name, spreads that mass re-auth too, and losing a session resets nothing about it.
