The slot pool meters the stream

A stream holds one php-fpm child, one of the site's fixed pool of PHP processes, for its whole life. Past the pool the platform queues each request for a worker and refuses it with 429 when none frees in time, so the pool caps streams before the platform steps in.

One stream's lease, from acquire to expiry

A slot's states

Acquire, check and touch fail closed: with no cache backend answering, ownership is unverifiable and every stream is refused. Release fails open, because a lease expires on its own.

What a refused browser does

A refusal is a 429 that fails the browser's EventSource outright, and the page reopens the stream under a backoff doubling from 2 s to a 30 s ceiling, so a tab the pool keeps refusing settles at one attempt every 30 s.

Why six

Six is the ceiling that leaves the site serving pages while fully subscribed. It is a budget, not a target: a host that reaches it regularly wants fewer dashboards or a larger allocation, because the headroom left over is what absorbs a traffic spike. A hub's pull of a spoke draws on the same six.
