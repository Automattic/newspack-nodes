A daemon that supervises itself, or a request that outlives its response

Tachikoma is a long-running process, and that process is the supervisor. Every worker here is a PHP request that exits within 595 seconds, so the whole lifecycle layer is new work with no Perl to consult, and so is everything that talks to WordPress.

Tachikoma a process

Supervision is the process. Peers are long-lived and can see each other, which is what lets Nodes/Broker.pm elect a leader per partition and rebalance when one falls behind.

Crash isolation is the fork. A Job that dies takes its own process and nothing else. Nodes/JobController.pm restarts one flagged to restart, no sooner than its 60-second $THROTTLE_DELAY after the last.

Newspack Nodes a request, 595 seconds at a time

There is no supervisor and no reaper. Cooperative_Stop::should_continue() owns every stop trigger, Worker_Base::execute()'s finally releases the lock and then self-respawns, Fleet_Node revives peers every 15 seconds, and Bootstrap::reconcile_fleet() on WP-Cron is the minute-cadence cold-start tier. Lock_Node is what makes the missing reaper safe.

There is no Job. Nothing forks: Job_Worker_Node dispatches each job inside its own worker, and a second process means another spawned request. Crash isolation comes from the dead-letter lifecycle rather than a process boundary. No broker either: partition ownership is what a topology declares, because an election needs peers that can see each other and the durable thing is the filesystem rather than the process.

WordPress is the sink

Hook_Node fires do_action / apply_filters on each VALUE, so a plugin reaches somebody else's topology without editing it, and Newspack_Log_Node fires newspack_log. Tachikoma's contract, none of its code.

A request owns the boundary, not the process

HTTP_In_Node, SSE_Out_Node, Log_Stream_Out_Node, SSE_In_Node, HTTP_Filter_Node and the *_CI_Node service interpreters live and die with one REST request, where Nodes/Socket.pm and the HTTP_* family hold a descriptor for the process's whole life. That is why HTTP_Out_Node borrows one clause from Socket.pm and nothing else.

Composition of our own

The async-job path, Job_Worker_Node, Job_Intake and Job_Delay, exists because there is no Job to fork into; Job_Probe_Node sweeps them the way TopicProbe.pm sweeps consumers. Tap_Node, Tail_Node, Remote_Link_Node / Remote_Source_Node, Settings_Sync_Node and Line_Fitter, a static PIPE_BUF fitter rather than a node, have no Perl behind them; Value_Timeout_Node's save_state() / restore_state() pair is a snapshot seam on a ported node, the one stock node an operator can name in add_snapshot_node without a subclass.
