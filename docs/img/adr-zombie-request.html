A worker is an HTTP request whose caller hangs up first

The platform caps a request at 15 minutes and offers no resident process. So the caller abandons the spawn POST after 250 ms, the endpoint survives the abort, and the process holding that connection is the worker for ~595 seconds, until it releases its lock and POSTs its own successor.

Rejected: fastcgi_finish_request()

Called ahead of the WP_REST_Response, which COMPLETES the response, it hands WordPress one it can no longer send: an empty body and "headers already sent" in the log, unnoticed because every caller discards the body anyway. Releasing the connection early buys nothing, since no caller waits for it.

Rejected: a resident daemon, or rescue-only restart

A daemon is unavailable on the platform. Without self-respawn, every ~10-minute recycle would idle the slot until a peer's stale-lock rescue; self-respawn hands off immediately and leaves the peer scan as the safety net (ADR-9), not the scheduler.

What it costs

Correctness rests on flawless offsetlog resume across ~144 respawns a day per worker, and on release-before-respawn. Reversed, the successor's acquire hits the still-held lock and skips. Revisit if the platform lifts the time cap or offers resident workers: the dance collapses into a normal long-lived loop.
