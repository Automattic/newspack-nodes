Four things can happen to a record at the 4 KB line

POSIX keeps an append-mode write() of at most PIPE_BUF (4096 bytes) from interleaving, so the firehose skips a lock on the common path. A record that will not fit is dropped whole, trimmed at the producer, or written under one of two opt-ins that lift the ceiling to 32 MiB.

A record reaches Partition::fill()

Measured as its packed bytes plus the newline Partition appends. JSON escapes a multibyte character to six bytes and an astral one to twelve, so the VALUE's own length is only a proxy.

packed + 1 > MAX_LINE_SIZE (4096)?
no: appended, lock-free, atomic  ·  yes: dropped (right) or one of the three routes below
Fits. One write(), no lock, no interleave.
Does not fit, no opt-in. Dropped with a rate-limited WARNING:, because half a record desyncs every reader after it.

Keeps the cap

Line_Fitter::fit( $message, $fields ) 4096

Runs at the producer, immediately before the sink. $fields is a sacrifice order, most expendable first: the first field is halved until the packed line plus newline fits or the field is spent, and only then does the next open. Fields outside the list are never touched.

Returns null when no listed field is left to cut, and the caller drops loud. A fitted line keeps its head; an unfitted one is gone.

Job_Probe_Node fits every jobstats record; event-logger-nodes fits its error entries, completed-request summaries and in-flight gyroscope rows.

The cap held rather than torn 4096

The default. A producer that outgrows 4 KB loses every oversize record until it fits or opts in. Callers must know their payload size.

Lifts the cap: enforced

allow_large_writes( $max_wait_ms, $debounce_ms ) 32 MiB

Takes a Lock at {partition_dir}/write.lock.d/. Acquisition retries every 100 ms up to max_wait_ms (DEFAULT_LOCK_WAIT_MS, 15 s), steals a lock dir that is orphaned or whose heartbeat has aged past LOCK_STALE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS (60 s), and throws at timeout.

15 s is sized for external writers with no loop to come back to: a page render through pyrobase's Log runtime, wp nodes ingest. It does not outwait a crashed predecessor, because sitting through the 60 s stale window blocks longer than the lease a caller inside a drain loop works under, and that caller stops heartbeating and has its own lock stolen while alive. Topology workers pass 0 (try-lock) or a debounce and retry next tick.

debounce_ms > 0 takes the lock per write burst and frees it after that much quiet, so intermittent large writers take turns. Still enforced.

Lifts the cap: asserted

void_warranty() 32 MiB

No lock, no heartbeat: the caller asserts it is the sole writer. For partitions already inside a single-writer boundary, such as a worker's own offsetlog under its topology lock or a per-worker durable log, where a second lock would only re-prove what the topology lock guarantees.

Two concurrent writers + void_warranty() = silent torn-write corruption. When in doubt, take the enforcing form.

Every large write is only as safe as the exclusivity behind it: 32 MiB (MAX_LARGE_LINE_SIZE) is far past atomicity.

One mode, not two flags

NONEcap 4096, no lock
a refused acquisition puts the cap all the way back down, a prior void included
LOCKcap 32 MiB, lock held or debounced
taking the lock supersedes a voided warranty
VOIDcap 32 MiB, on the caller's word

A lifted cap with no lock behind it is the corruption both opt-ins exist to bound, so the unwind must never arrive at it. Topic_Node carries the same three modes and applies whichever is set to every partition it materializes, so a topic-wide producer opts in once.

Habitable zone

Everything under base_directory — locks, partitions, offsets, IPC — is scoped to one host's local POSIX filesystem, shared by that host's PHP processes. NFS and overlay mounts void the 4 KB append atomicity; containers with separate /tmp are separate hosts, and two containers pointed at one path that is two filesystems split-brain silently. Nothing detects an exotic mount at runtime, because no portable signal identifies one on the platforms we run on.
